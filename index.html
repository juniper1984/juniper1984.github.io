About the Project

Millions of rural Americans have no access to skin cancer screening

In rural America, millions of people live hours from the nearest dermatologist. Many counties have no dermatologist at all. Skin cancer is the most common cancer in the United States, and early detection is the single biggest factor in survival — but early detection requires access to screening that simply doesn't exist in large parts of the country.

Rural primary care providers do their best, but they aren't trained to differentiate a benign mole from an early melanoma. Patients face long wait times for specialist referrals, significant travel distances, and costs that push many to delay care until symptoms are impossible to ignore. By then, outcomes are dramatically worse.

AI-assisted screening has shown real promise in research settings, but existing tools require cloud connectivity, expensive proprietary hardware, and ongoing subscription fees. They're designed for well-funded urban practices, not the rural clinics and community health centers where the gap is widest.

Edge Diagnostic Research is a one-person research nonprofit working to close that gap. I'm building a portable skin lesion classifier that runs entirely on-device using low-cost, commercially available hardware — a tool that a rural primary care office, a community health worker, or a free clinic could realistically deploy without internet access, vendor contracts, or specialized training.

This is early-stage work. I have a working prototype and a trained model. What comes next is improving the model, diversifying the training data, and finding clinical partners to validate the device against expert diagnosis.

Current Work

Where we are and what's next

Working Prototype

A self-contained device built on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo-8L AI accelerator. Runs skin lesion classification in real time, fully on-device, using low-cost, off-the-shelf components with no cloud dependency.

Initial Model Trained

Classification model trained on publicly available skin lesion datasets (ISIC/HAM10000). Functional across seven lesion categories, with known limitations in skin tone diversity that we're working to address.

Dataset Diversification

Seeking clinical partners to build a more representative training dataset — particularly images of darker skin tones captured under real-world conditions with the device's own optics.

Clinical Validation

Planning a prospective study to compare device classifications against expert dermatologist diagnosis. Looking for academic partners interested in collaborating on this work.

Commitment to Openness

Open by design

Everything produced by this project — software, trained models, datasets, and hardware designs — will be published under open licenses for noncommercial use. If this tool can help save lives, it shouldn't be locked behind a paywall.

Collaborate

Help bring screening to the communities that need it

I started this project because I saw a problem that technology should be able to solve — people in rural America going unscreened for skin cancer simply because they live too far from a specialist. I'm a software engineer, not a clinician, and getting from a working prototype to a tool that can make a real difference in someone's life requires expertise I don't have alone. If you're a dermatologist, a rural health researcher, a clinical data specialist, or someone who works in the communities this is built for, I'd welcome a conversation about how we can work together.
